AGILE SPRINT LAB
120 minute rundown and facilitator script
IAF conference in China

This script supports an in person workshop for facilitators, consultants, and coaches. Participants form teams of four or five, bring challenges from their work in the age of AI, and develop solutions together.

The central process is sequential. Each team selects one challenge, works on its solution, captures the completed solution on one poster, and checks its acceptance criteria and the workshop Definition of Done. If time remains, the team selects the next challenge and repeats. A team may complete several posters. The work period ends at the same time for every team.

Read the spoken paragraphs aloud. Instructions in square brackets are delivery cues. Timing includes participant activities, movement, explanations, and transitions. Replace [your name] before rehearsal.

RUNDOWN

00-07   Welcome and purpose                                  7 minutes
07-19   Introduction cards and skills marketplace           12 minutes
19-24   Team roles and working agreements                    5 minutes
24-33   Challenge cards and readiness                        9 minutes
33-47   Share and clarify challenges                        14 minutes
47-50   Explain the Definition of Done                       3 minutes
50-58   Map and prioritize challenges                        8 minutes
58-63   Agree on the first goal and approach                 5 minutes
63-90   Work on challenges one at a time                     27 minutes
90-95   Finish capture and prepare for review                5 minutes
95-110  Gallery walk and response to feedback               15 minutes

117-120 Closing                                              3 minutes

The five stage arc runs through these activities. Opening establishes the purpose and teams. Diverging gives every participant space to express a challenge. Organizing makes the challenges understandable and visible. Converging helps teams choose and complete work. Closing supports review, reflection, and learning.

1  WELCOME AND PURPOSE
00-07

[Display the workshop title. Welcome participants.]

Hello, everyone. My name is [your name], and I am an Agile Coach. Thank you for joining me today.

In this workshop, I will share some practical elements from the Agile world that I have found useful in my work. Each element serves a purpose. Together, they help a team understand challenges, choose what to work on, and create useful results.

I originally designed this as a full day workshop, where a large group works together as one team. For this conference, I have adapted it to our 120 minute timebox. A timebox means a fixed amount of time.

Today, you will work in teams of four or five. Most of your discussion will happen within your team. Later, you will explore the other teams' work.

Our topic is the challenges we face as facilitators, consultants, and coaches in the age of AI. Think back a few years. Did you expect AI to become part of your work in the way it has today?

[Pause for reflection. Take at most two short responses if time allows.]

AI can help us, and it can also change the challenges we face. Today, you will bring one challenge from your own experience and explore it with your team.

We will move through five stages: opening, diverging, organizing, converging, and closing. As we go, I will explain the Agile elements behind the activities.

You will experience the workshop as participants and reflect on it as facilitators. Pay attention to how each activity helps your team work together.

Before we form teams, let us agree on language. I want everyone to feel comfortable taking part. Within your team, you may use the language or languages that work for you. Please check that everyone can understand and join the discussion.

When we share with other teams or with the whole room, we will use English as our common language. Help each other prepare and explain your ideas. Simple English is enough. Your team needs a way to share in English; you can support each other with translation.

2  INTRODUCTION CARDS AND SKILLS MARKETPLACE
07-19

[Distribute introduction cards. Display the four fields and the strength examples in Appendix A. Use the first three minutes for instructions and writing, the next five for meeting people, and the final four for settling into teams, checking language arrangements, and explaining self-selection.]

We will begin with a skills marketplace. This is a chance to meet people and introduce what you can bring to a team.

Please fill in four things on your card: what you would like us to call you, one or two strengths you bring, the languages you can work in, and the language you prefer for discussion.

Your contribution could be listening, asking useful questions, organizing ideas, or explaining things clearly. Choose something that feels natural to you. The slide gives you more examples.

Write clearly so other people can read your card. You have two minutes.

[Start the timer. When writing time ends, invite movement. Offer a seated option for anyone who needs it.]

Now, move around and introduce yourself. Please meet at least three people before settling into a team. If possible, meet someone you do not already know.

Look for different strengths. Also, talk about the languages you can use together. We have five minutes for meeting people, followed by four minutes to finish forming teams.

[Announce the final four minutes.]

Please form a team of four or five and find a place to work. Check that everyone can join your discussion and that your team has a way to share key points in English. Agree on any translation support together.

If you need help finding a team, please let me know.

[Help participants find a group. For uneven numbers, adjust group sizes so nobody is excluded.]

You have just experienced self-selection. You chose your teammates within a few shared conditions. This gives you a part in shaping your team and a responsibility to help it work well.

3  TEAM ROLES AND WORKING AGREEMENTS
19-24

[Use the first two minutes to explain and choose roles. Use the remaining three minutes to introduce and agree on working agreements. Give the team facilitator a visible token or marker.]

Please choose one person to be your team facilitator. This person will help the team stay within the time, hear every voice, and check that completed work meets our agreed conditions.

The token helps us recognize that role. The facilitator may also contribute ideas. Their responsibility is to support the team's process.

Also, agree on who will capture your discussion and who will help the team communicate its results. You can share or change these responsibilities.

Next, agree on two or three ways you want to work together today. Ask: what do we need from each other to work well?

You might agree to hear from everyone, ask when something is unclear, and help each other stay focused. Include your language agreement so everyone can take part.

Write your agreements where you can see them. Check that everyone can support them.

[At the end, check that each team has a facilitator and visible agreements.]

4  CHALLENGE CARDS AND READINESS
24-33

[Show the challenge card. Use three minutes for explanation, three for individual writing, and three for each person to check and improve their own card.]

Now, each person will write one challenge from their work as a facilitator, consultant, or coach in the age of AI.

On the front, complete three sentences:

When I am...
The challenge I face is...
This affects my work by...

Describe a situation you have experienced. Keep it specific enough for your team to understand and work on today.

On the back, write what you hope to receive from today's discussion. What would make a solution useful to you? Write one or two clear conditions. We call these acceptance criteria.

For example, you might need a short checklist for reviewing an AI-generated workshop agenda, with questions that help you spot missing information. The acceptance criteria help the team understand what a useful response should contain.

We will also use a Definition of Ready. For this workshop, a challenge is ready when its situation, challenge, and impact are clear, its acceptance criteria are understandable, and the team can make useful progress within today's time.

Definition of Ready is a practice some Agile teams use. Here, it helps us start with a shared understanding.

Please write quietly on your own first. This gives every person space to express their experience before the group discussion. You have three minutes.

[Ask team facilitators to support quiet writing. After three minutes, allow three minutes for checking and clarifying individual cards.]

Read your card again. Could someone else understand it? Are your acceptance criteria something the team can address today? You may ask for help if a phrase is unclear.

5  SHARE AND CLARIFY CHALLENGES
33-47

[Each participant has up to two minutes to explain the front and back of their card. Use the remaining time for clarifying questions.]

Please take turns sharing your challenge and acceptance criteria. Give each person up to two minutes. Listen first, then ask questions to understand.

At this point, help each other clarify the challenges. We will choose what to work on in the next step.

The person who brought the challenge can explain the context and improve the wording. Keep the focus on the situation, its effect, and what a useful result would look like.

Team facilitators, please make sure everyone has a turn. Before moving on, check that your team understands each card well enough to choose.

[Circulate. Help teams narrow challenges that are too broad.]

You have been making the challenges clearer. This has a connection to refinement in Agile work: we add useful detail so people can understand the work and make decisions about it.

6  DEFINITION OF DONE
47-50

[Display the completion checklist before teams prioritize.]

Before you choose a challenge, let us agree on what completed work should look like. In Scrum, the Definition of Done gives people a shared understanding of the quality expected in completed work. We are adapting that idea for today's workshop.

For each challenge you complete, create one poster. It must show the challenge, its acceptance criteria, and your team's solution. Write clearly and make it large enough for other people to read.

Check that your solution addresses the acceptance criteria. Show how it meets them. If it does not meet them yet, keep working on that challenge.

Your solution might be an approach, a checklist, a set of steps, or a practical response to the situation. Make it specific enough for the person who brought the challenge to use or try.

When the solution is complete and captured on its poster, your team may choose the next challenge. Work on one challenge at a time and repeat this process while time remains.

We will photograph completed posters for sharing. If the website is available, upload them using the instructions on the slide. We will use the physical posters for our gallery walk, so everyone can review the work even if an upload fails.

[Local workshop Definition of Done: the poster identifies the challenge and acceptance criteria; presents a usable solution; explains how it meets the criteria; and is readable for other teams. Photograph and upload are documentation steps.]

7  MAP AND PRIORITIZE CHALLENGES
50-58

[Give each team a matrix with red-dot count on the horizontal axis and green-dot count on the vertical axis. Label counts from zero to five. Provide red and green dots marked R and G so color is not the only cue.]

We will use two types of dots to help your team decide where to start.

A red dot means: I have experienced this challenge too. You may place one red dot on each challenge you have personally experienced, including your own.

A green dot means: I want our team to work on this challenge today. Each person has one green dot. Use it to show your first choice.

Please vote quietly first. Then count the dots on each card.

On your matrix, more red dots move a challenge further to the right. More green dots move it higher. Place cards at the matching counts. If cards have the same counts, place them beside each other.

Look at the pattern together. Which challenges are shared? Which ones do people want to work on? Check the scope and acceptance criteria as well.

The dots help you have a discussion and choose. Agree on your first challenge and a possible order for the remaining challenges. If there is a tie, ask which challenge you can make useful progress on within the time available.

[Within this eight-minute block, use about two minutes for instructions, one to vote, two to plot, and three to discuss and choose.]

Keep the remaining cards visible. Once you finish your first challenge, return to them and choose the next one. You may change the order if your team learns something new.

8  AGREE ON THE FIRST GOAL AND APPROACH
58-63

[Ask teams to make a short plan for the selected challenge.]

Before you start, agree on what you want to achieve and how you will work.

First, what useful result do you want for this challenge? Second, what will you put on the poster? Third, how will you use your time to create it?

These questions connect to the purpose of Sprint Planning: understanding why the work matters, what to do, and how to do it.

The person who brought the challenge can explain the experience in more detail. Other people may bring a related experience, a tool, an example, or useful questions.

This is different from your introduction card. Earlier, you shared your general strengths. Now, consider what you can contribute to this particular challenge.

Agree on who will help capture the solution. Your facilitator will help the team hear each person and check progress. You have five minutes to prepare your first approach.

9  WORK ON CHALLENGES ONE AT A TIME
63-90

[Start a visible 27-minute timer. Display the repeated sequence: choose, plan, solve, capture, check, choose next. Circulate without taking over team decisions. Teams may take a brief stretch as needed within this timebox.]

You now have 27 minutes to work together.

Work on your selected challenge. Develop a solution that meets its acceptance criteria, capture it on one poster, and check the Definition of Done.

Once that challenge is complete, look at the time remaining. If there is enough time, choose your next challenge, make a short plan, and repeat.

Your team may complete more than one poster. Each poster represents one completed challenge. The number of posters will depend on the challenges you choose and the time you need.

Team facilitators, help everyone contribute. Keep the acceptance criteria visible and check whether the discussion is moving toward a useful result.

[At minute 80, announce ten minutes remaining.]

You have ten minutes left. Check where you are against your acceptance criteria. Decide how to use the remaining time.

[At minute 87, announce three minutes remaining.]

You have three minutes left. Please focus on completing and capturing the challenge you are working on. If it will remain unfinished, make its current status clear.

[Useful prompts while circulating: What does the challenge owner need? Which acceptance criterion are you addressing? What would make this usable? Who has a different view? What still needs to be completed?]

10  FINISH CAPTURE AND PREPARE FOR REVIEW
90-95

[Allow five minutes for checking, display, and photographs. Avoid introducing a new challenge.]

Our main work period has ended. Please use these five minutes to check your posters and prepare them for others to see.

Keep the challenge, acceptance criteria, and solution visible together. Check the writing and mark each poster with your team name or number.

Display every completed poster. If a challenge is unfinished, label it clearly as work in progress so visitors understand its status.

Take a clear photo of each completed poster. If you can access the website, upload it now. If you cannot, keep the poster displayed and the photo saved. We can still review the work together.

Choose one person to stay with your posters for the first part of the gallery walk. You will switch halfway through so everyone can visit other teams.

11  GALLERY WALK AND RESPONSE TO FEEDBACK
95-110

[Use two minutes for instructions, five for the first visit period, five for the second, and three for teams to return and consider feedback. Supply sticky notes.]

We will now have a gallery walk. Visit the other teams and explore their posters.

Look at the original challenge, the acceptance criteria, and the solution. Ask questions to understand their thinking.

Leave a short note: what is useful here, or what question or suggestion could help improve it? Keep your feedback clear and respectful.

Please use simple English when communicating across teams. You can help each other explain or translate. Shared feedback notes should be understandable to the receiving team, with a short English version where needed.

One person from each team will stay with the posters for the first five minutes. Then we will switch, so that person also has time to visit other teams.

[After the first five-minute visit period, announce the switch. After the second, ask everyone to return to their own team.]

Please return to your team and read the feedback. What did other people notice? Is there anything you want to clarify, improve, or test next? You have three minutes to discuss.

This connects to review in Agile work. We make results visible, inspect them together, and use what we learn to decide what to do next.

12  TEAM RETROSPECTIVE AND FEEDBACK WALL
110-117

[Include the short instructions within the seven-minute block. Use about one minute for individual reflection, three for team discussion, and three for writing and posting personal feedback.]

Now, let us reflect on how your team worked together.

Take one quiet minute to think about two questions: what helped us work well together, and what would we change next time?

Then share your thoughts with your team. Choose one change you would try the next time you work together.

[After the team discussion, distribute two sticky notes per participant.]

Please write two short notes for our feedback wall.

On the first, write one message for me about this workshop. What helped you, or what could I improve?

On the second, write one practice you will take into your own work and where you would like to try it.

Post them in the matching sections of the wall. You may use a language you are comfortable with. A short English translation is welcome if you can add one.

13  CLOSING
117-120

[Invite at most two brief takeaways if time allows.]

Today, you formed your own teams, made your language needs visible, and agreed on how to work together.

You made challenges clear, chose where to start, agreed on what completed work should look like, and created solutions one challenge at a time.

You also reviewed other teams' work and reflected on your own collaboration.

These activities show how selected Agile elements can support a workshop. Each element has a purpose, and you can adapt it to the people and situation you are working with.

As you leave, choose one practice you would like to try. Where could it help in your next session?

Thank you for your ideas, your participation, and the support you gave each other today.

APPENDIX C  DELIVERY NOTES

Prepare enough poster paper for more than one completed challenge per team. For 30 to 40 participants, expect roughly 6 to 10 teams. Have spare sheets available throughout the work period.

Use introduction cards large enough to read while meeting people. Prepare separate challenge cards with space on both sides. Keep both sides available when producing the solution poster, by copying the acceptance criteria onto it or attaching the card so both are readable.

Prepare thick markers, sticky notes, red and green dots with letter labels, facilitator role tokens, tape, a visible timer, and an area for the feedback wall. Photograph and test the website upload path before the event, while keeping the physical gallery ready.

During prioritization, offer one brief clarification of confusing wording. Keep voting quiet until everyone has chosen, then discuss the results. A challenge being widely shared does not by itself determine its priority.

During the work period, each team chooses its own pace. Do not impose synchronized rounds. Keep the common finishing time visible. When one challenge meets its criteria and is captured, the team can move to its next challenge.

If time becomes tight, reduce whole-room responses and optional explanatory examples. Protect the main collaboration time, the gallery review, and the final reflection. Do not cut the requirement to hear each participant's challenge.

The three role responsibilities are flexible. The team facilitator supports the process and can contribute. The challenge owner clarifies the need and helps check whether the response addresses it. Everyone shares responsibility for the work.

The conference session adapts selected Agile and Scrum ideas. Self-selection, working agreements, Definition of Ready, voting, and the gallery walk are facilitation practices rather than prescribed Scrum requirements. A poster is the workshop deliverable. Its acceptance criteria describe the usefulness of the response today; any real-world impact needs to be checked through later use or testing.

Reference for the Scrum connections: The Scrum Guide, November 2020, https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html

APPENDIX D  USEFUL PHRASES

To invite another voice: Let us hear from someone who has not spoken yet.
To clarify: Could you give us a specific example?
To return to the challenge: How does this help us address the challenge on the card?
To check usefulness: Which acceptance criterion does this address?
To manage time: We have five minutes left. What can we complete in that time?
To support language needs: Could someone help explain that so everyone can follow?
To acknowledge uncertainty: What would you need to check before trying this in your own work?
To move to the next challenge: This poster is complete. Check the time and choose your next challenge.
